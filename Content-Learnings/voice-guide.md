Voice/style guide the LinkedIn Writer drafts from. **This is a default, not
a personalized profile** — no posts have been approved or edited yet, so
there is no real signal to learn from. Once Phase 7 (approval workflow) has
accumulated approved-unedited posts and heavily-edited posts, this document
should be updated to reflect what actually got approved, not this generic
starting point.

## Hook style

- Open with a specific, concrete claim or tension — not a question that
  could apply to any post ("Ever wondered how X works?").
- No throat-clearing. First line should be able to stand alone as the post.

## Structure

- Hook → the actual useful content, scannable in short paragraphs or a
  tight list → a natural discussion question at the end, only when there's
  a genuine reason to ask one (not appended out of habit).
- Line breaks between thoughts, not walls of text. LinkedIn renders short
  paragraphs well.

## Length

- Default target: comfortably under LinkedIn's ~3,000 character limit —
  aim for roughly 1,200–1,500 characters unless the material genuinely
  needs more room (e.g. a step-by-step tutorial).

## Tone

- Conversational and direct, like explaining something to a smart peer —
  not a press release, not a motivational poster.
- Technically accurate over impressive-sounding. If something is uncertain,
  say so plainly rather than asserting confidently.

## Avoid (common AI-writing tells)

- Opening with "In today's fast-paced world...", "Let's dive in", "Imagine
  a world where...".
- Rhetorical-question hooks that could preface literally any post.
- Excessive emoji as bullet substitutes (🚀 🔥 💡 stacked at line starts).
- Generic CTAs with no real question attached ("Thoughts? Let me know
  below!" with nothing specific to react to).
- Em-dash overuse as a substitute for real sentence structure.
- Hedging every sentence ("It's important to note that...", "One could
  argue...").

## Hashtags

- 3–5 per post, directly tied to the topic/category — never stuffed, never
  generic filler tags unrelated to the actual content.

## CTA preferences

- A closing question only when the post's content naturally raises one.
  Otherwise, end on the substance — a forced CTA reads as generic.

## Emoji usage

- Sparing. Fine as an occasional visual anchor (e.g. one at the very
  start), not as a formatting crutch throughout the body.
