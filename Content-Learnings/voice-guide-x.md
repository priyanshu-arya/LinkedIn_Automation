Voice/style guide `write-draft-x` drafts from. **This is a default, not a
personalized profile** — no X posts have been approved or edited yet.
Update this once approved/edited posts exist to learn from, same discipline
as `voice-guide.md`.

## Hook style

- Line 1 is the whole pitch — X gives you less room than LinkedIn to warm
  up. A specific claim, number, or tension, not a scene-setter.
- No throat-clearing, no "🧵" announcement needed if the first tweet already
  reads as worth continuing.

## Structure

- **Single post:** one idea, stated tightly, done. Don't pad to look
  substantial.
- **Thread:** hook tweet → one idea per subsequent tweet (short, each one
  should stand alone if quote-tweeted) → a closing tweet that lands the
  point, not a recap.

## Length

- Single post: well under X's ~280 characters per tweet (or the long-post
  limit if the account has it) — write tight, don't fill the ceiling.
- Thread: no fixed cap on tweet count; stop when the idea is actually done,
  not at a round number.

## Tone

- Terser and more opinionated than LinkedIn's voice — X rewards a clear
  stance stated plainly, not hedged professionalism.
- Still technically accurate over impressive-sounding — a sharp tone is not
  license to overstate a claim the research doesn't support.

## Avoid (common AI-writing tells)

- Same list as `voice-guide.md`'s Avoid section, plus: thread-numbering
  filler ("1/", "🧵 a thread on...") used as a substitute for an actual
  hook, and a final tweet that just says "Thoughts?" with nothing specific
  to react to.

## Hashtags

- 1–2 per post, only if genuinely useful for discovery — X hashtag culture
  is much lighter than LinkedIn's; default to none over stuffing.

## CTA preferences

- A closing question only when it's genuinely specific to the point just
  made — same rule as LinkedIn, just even less tolerance for a generic one.

## Emoji usage

- Sparing, same as LinkedIn — an occasional anchor, never a formatting
  crutch or thread-numbering device.
