Voice/style guide for both `write-draft-substack-article` and
`write-draft-substack-note`. **This is a default, not a personalized
profile** — no Substack content has been approved or edited yet. Update
this once approved/edited posts exist to learn from, same discipline as
`voice-guide.md`.

## Articles (long-form)

### Hook style

- The opening paragraph should earn the reader's time investment — a
  concrete stake or question the piece will actually resolve, not a
  scene-setting intro. Subscribers already opted in; don't waste that with
  a generic preamble.

### Structure

- Real sections with headings, not one continuous essay — a reader should
  be able to skim headings and get the shape of the argument.
- Depth is the point here, unlike LinkedIn/X: it's fine (expected) to go
  deeper into mechanism, caveats, and nuance than a feed post ever could.
- End with an actual takeaway or point of view, not a summary restating
  what was just said.

### Length

- No fixed ceiling — governed by the material, not a target word count.
  A thin topic stretched to look substantial reads as padding; say what's
  true in however much space it takes.

### Tone

- More essay-like and personal than LinkedIn's peer-explaining-to-peer
  tone — first person, willing to argue a position at length, still
  technically accurate over impressive-sounding.

### SEO

- `seo_description` and `seo_tags` should reflect what the piece is
  actually about, not keyword-stuffed guesses — they exist for search/share
  previews, not to game anything.

## Notes (short-form, Substack's own social feed)

- Same brevity discipline as `voice-guide-x.md`'s single-post section, but
  the tone can be more casual/off-the-cuff — Notes read as a running
  conversation with subscribers, not a polished feed post.
- No hashtag convention on Substack Notes — omit them entirely.

## Avoid (both formats)

- Same AI-writing-tells list as `voice-guide.md`'s Avoid section
  ("In today's fast-paced world...", rhetorical-question hooks, emoji
  stacking, em-dash overuse, generic CTAs, hedging every sentence).
